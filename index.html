How Prop Score works

The score (1–99) ranks how strong a play looks from several angles at once, not just how mispriced it is. For both the over and the under, the model starts from recent form (last 10, last 5, season) pulled toward what the market says, then adjusts for:

  • Projection: the shape of his recent games against the line, using the middle game (median) so one huge game doesn't inflate it.
  • Streaks and usage: hot or cold runs, and whether touches or targets are rising.
  • Matchup: how the opponent's defense treats that position and stat, plus past games against them.
  • Game script: the spread and total. Big implied team totals lift everything; underdogs throw more and favorites run more.
  • Team style and weather: pass-heavy or run-heavy offense, wind, rain, and cold. Indoor games get no weather effect.
  • Under lean: unders have historically cashed more often because the public bets overs, so unders get a small nudge.
  • Price: the estimated hit chance versus the chance the price implies.

Value counts most, but plays where several of these agree score higher. Open any player to see the full breakdown.

Props are hidden unless the player has played 7 of the last 10 weeks, has a real role (targets, carries, or attempts), the line isn't tiny, and the price isn't juiced worse than about −255. Tap Show under the list to see them.

It's a research shortcut, not a prediction. The books see most of this too, so check the chart and matchup before you bet.
